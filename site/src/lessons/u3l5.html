Having built the pieces yourself in lessons 1–4, you now know enough to make the real decision: for a production chain, you almost certainly shouldn't build those pieces from scratch. You should use a framework, and choose which one based on what you're optimising for. This lesson maps the major stacks to the situations they fit, so the choice is deliberate rather than defaulted.

The decision, in one paragraph

It comes down to one question: do you want to inherit Ethereum's security and compose with its DeFi, or do you need control that only your own chain can give?

  • Want composability with Ethereum DeFi and its users? Use a rollup stack (OP Stack, Orbit). You inherit Ethereum security, get EVM tooling, launch fast, and your contracts can interact with the broader ecosystem. The cost is the rollup's constraints: a sequencer, the data-availability choices of Unit 4, and L1-bound finality.
  • Need a custom matching engine, cancel priority, sub-second blocks? Use an appchain (Cosmos SDK, or a bespoke Rust chain on Commonware). You control ordering and performance end to end (Unit 2's appchain case), at the cost of bootstrapping your own validator set and security.

The Hyperliquid pattern

If you're building a perp DEX, study how Hyperliquid resolved the apparent either/or. It paired a native order-book engine (HyperCore) for speed and control with an EVM (HyperEVM) so third parties can still deploy composable contracts. You don't always have to choose between "fast custom engine" and "composable EVM"; the sophisticated answer is often both, with the native engine for the hot path and a VM alongside it for the ecosystem (Unit 3 lesson 4, Unit 9).

Build it

Spin up a local OP Stack devnet (or an Arbitrum Orbit devnode) and a Cosmos SDK chain scaffolded with Ignite. Deploy the same counter app to both, and write down the developer experience, the block times, and the trust assumptions of each. Doing the same thing on a rollup and an appchain, and feeling the difference (the rollup inherits security but constrains you; the appchain frees you but makes security your problem), turns the table above from abstract advice into a decision you've actually made with your hands.
