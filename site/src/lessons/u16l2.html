Every protocol publishes a headline number designed to look impressive, and most of those numbers can be inflated or are measuring the wrong thing. Learning which metrics are real, which lie, and how, is the difference between evaluating a protocol and being marketed to. This lesson is a field guide to the standard metrics and the ratios that let you compare venues honestly, with the one number, earnings, that's hardest to fake and least often shown.

Earnings is the honest number

Here's the metric that cuts through the marketing. A protocol can show large fees and revenue while paying out more than that in token incentives to attract the activity that generated them. Earnings, revenue minus token incentives, is what's actually left, and for many "successful" protocols it's negative: they're buying their revenue with token emissions (Unit 13). A protocol with high revenue and negative earnings is subsidising its own activity and will shrink when the subsidy stops. Always compute earnings; it's the number that tells you whether the business works.

The comparison ratios

take rate = protocol revenue / volume (e.g. bps per $ traded) capital efficiency = volume / TVL (or fees / TVL) volume/OI ratio far above peers → suspect wash trading

Take rate compares how much each venue extracts per dollar traded; capital efficiency compares how hard each venue's TVL works; an anomalous volume/OI ratio is a wash-trading tell (Unit 9).

These ratios let you compare venues that look different on absolute numbers. A venue with huge volume but a volume/OI ratio ten times its peers isn't ten times busier; it's probably washing (Unit 9's Aster example). A venue with modest TVL but high fees/TVL is working its capital hard, which is usually a better sign than a large idle TVL.

Build it

Pick 5 DEXs or perp venues. Pull fees, revenue, incentives, volume and TVL for 6 months, compute earnings, take rate and fees/TVL, and rank them. Then write which ones are actually profitable. The ranking by earnings will often look very different from the ranking by TVL or volume that the ecosystem talks about, and seeing a "top" protocol with deeply negative earnings, or a quiet one that actually makes money, is the lesson that inoculates you against headline metrics for good.
