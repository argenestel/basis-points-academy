Classic liquidation is a cliff: your health factor crosses a line and your collateral is seized in one discrete, penalised event (Unit 7). crvUSD replaced the cliff with a slope. Its LLAMMA mechanism spreads your liquidation across a range of prices, converting collateral gradually as the price falls and converting it back if the price recovers. This lesson is how soft liquidation works and the trade-off it makes: no cliff and no big penalty, but a slow bleed in choppy markets.

From cliff to slope

crvUSD (Curve, May 2023) uses the Lending-Liquidating AMM Algorithm, LLAMMA:

  • Your collateral is spread across N bands, which are price ranges just below the current price.
  • When the market price falls into your bands, LLAMMA becomes an AMM that sells your collateral for crvUSD, band by band. Arbitrageurs do these trades because LLAMMA's price is offset to make them profitable (Unit 6's LVR, used here on purpose).
  • If the price recovers, the same mechanism runs in reverse, buying your collateral back: de-liquidation. You come out the other side still holding (most of) your collateral.
  • Only if your health keeps falling below zero do you face a classic hard liquidation.
loss while in soft liquidation ≈ a small % per band crossed (like LVR: you trade against arbitrageurs) choosing N: more bands = gentler conversion, but positions enter soft liquidation earlier

The cost of soft liquidation is the LVR you pay to the arbitrageurs who do the band-by-band conversions. It's small per band but accumulates if the price chops back and forth across your bands.

The peg and reuse

crvUSD's peg is defended by PegKeepers that mint or burn crvUSD into stableswap pools when the price drifts (Unit 8's par mechanism), and by a borrow rate that rises when crvUSD trades below $1. LlamaLend (2024) reused LLAMMA for isolated lending markets, so the soft-liquidation mechanism isn't just for the stablecoin; it's a general lending primitive.

The trade-off

Soft liquidation's advantages: no cliff, no big penalty, and liquidation capacity built into the protocol (the AMM always has a bid). Its cost: you bleed in choppy markets, because every time the price crosses your bands you pay LVR to the arbitrageurs, and a volatile sideways market can cross your bands many times. Compare it with Unit 1's liquidity spirals: a classic liquidation dumps all your collateral at one price (contributing to the cascade), while LLAMMA spreads the forced selling over a price range, which is gentler on the market but costlier to you if the market whipsaws. It's a different point on the same trade-off between protecting the borrower and protecting the system.

Build it

Simulate LLAMMA in Python with 10 bands: drive the price down 30% and back up along a choppy path, and compare the borrower's losses with a classic 5%-penalty liquidation on the same path. The choppy path is the key: on a smooth round trip, LLAMMA wins easily (you get your collateral back); on a choppy one, the accumulated band-crossing LVR can approach or exceed the classic penalty. Seeing both outcomes is how you understand when soft liquidation helps the borrower and when it quietly costs them more than a cliff would have.
