pump.fun turned token creation into a one-click act and, in doing so, reshaped Solana into the chain where anyone launches anything in seconds. The result was a fee business built on pure speculation, and a competition over who captures the flow. This lesson is the business of launchpads in 2026: how they make money, why almost nothing graduates, and what's actually durable in a market built on attention.

pump.fun and PumpSwap

pump.fun launched on Solana in January 2024. Anyone could create a token in seconds on a bonding curve (lesson 1), with no liquidity needed. Graduated tokens initially moved to Raydium, until March 2025, when pump.fun launched its own AMM, PumpSwap, and started sharing fees with creators. That was a strategic move: by owning the post-graduation venue, pump.fun captured the trading fees that previously went to Raydium.

The ~1% graduation rate is the key fact. The launchpad doesn't need tokens to succeed; it earns fees on the speculation itself, the buying and selling on the curve, whether or not the token ever graduates. It's a fee business on flow, and the flow is driven by attention, not by any token's fundamentals.

The economics

Launchpads are fee businesses on speculative flow, which has a specific shape:

  • Revenue scales with attention, and attention is cyclical. A launchpad booms when memecoins are hot and quiets when they're not.
  • The durable parts are distribution (being where users already are), post-graduation liquidity (owning the AMM, as PumpSwap does, so you keep earning after graduation), and creator incentives (fee sharing to attract the people who bring the attention).
  • The model spread: creator coins on Base (Zora), BNB Chain launchpads, "launch as a service" for apps. The one-click-launch primitive turned out to be broadly useful.

A fee business on attention

The honest description of a launchpad is a toll booth on speculation. It doesn't care whether any individual token is good; it earns on the volume of people trying. This is a real and large business (the PUMP sale raised hundreds of millions), but it's cyclical in a way that fundamental DeFi isn't, because its revenue is a function of how much attention memecoins are getting this month. The defensible moat isn't the launch mechanic, which is trivially forkable (LetsBonk forked it and briefly won), but the distribution and the post-graduation liquidity capture, which is why pump.fun built PumpSwap.

Build it

Using Dune or DefiLlama, chart for one launchpad over 90 days: tokens created per day, the graduation rate, and the post-graduation volume share. Estimate the launchpad's revenue per graduated token. The numbers will show you that the business is in the flow, not the graduates, most tokens die on the curve while the launchpad still earned its fees, and that owning the post-graduation venue (PumpSwap) is where the durable revenue is. That's the structural insight behind the whole category.
