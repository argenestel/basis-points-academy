Four ideas from traditional derivatives show up, barely disguised, in almost every DeFi protocol: the cost of carry, the basis trade, option convexity, and margin. Ethena is a carry trade. A perp is a future with the expiry removed. An LP position is a short option. A liquidation is a margin call executed by a bot. Learn the four with numbers once and you'll recognise them everywhere.

Futures and the cost of carry

A futures contract is an agreement to trade later at a price fixed now. Its fair price isn't a forecast; it's pinned to spot by arbitrage. If you can buy the asset today, finance it at rate r, and collect any yield q it pays while you hold it, then selling the future against it must earn you exactly nothing after costs, or someone will do it until it does.

F = S · e^((r − q) · T) basis = F − S positive: contango negative: backwardation

S spot, r your funding cost, q the yield the asset pays (staking, dividends), T time to expiry in years.

If the future trades at $2,040 instead, the cash-and-carry is: borrow $2,000, buy 1 ETH, stake it, sell one future at $2,040. At expiry you deliver the ETH, repay $2,025 (principal plus interest), and have earned $15 of staking. Profit: 2,040 − 2,025 + 15 = $30 with no price risk. Enough people doing this pushes the future back toward $2,010.

Perpetuals: a future that never converges

Expiry is what forces a future back to spot. A perpetual has none, so it needs a substitute: funding. Every hour (on most venues) longs pay shorts a rate proportional to how far the perp trades above spot, or shorts pay longs if it trades below. That payment is the carry, charged continuously instead of being baked into a price. Unit 9 derives the exact formulas; what matters here is the equivalence: holding a perp long and paying funding is economically the same as holding a future and paying the basis.

Which means the cash-and-carry works on perps too: buy spot, short the perp, collect funding. Ethena's USDe is this trade run at scale, with the funding income paid to sUSDe holders (Unit 8). When funding is positive the trade earns; when it turns negative for long enough, it bleeds. Everything you'll learn about USDe is a corollary of the table above with r and q swapped for funding.

Options and convexity

A call option pays max(S − K, 0) at expiry: nothing below the strike K, then dollar for dollar above it. That kink is convexity. The buyer's payoff curves upward, so they benefit from big moves in either direction once you include puts; the seller's curves downward. Black and Scholes showed the price depends on volatility, which is why options traders say they trade vol, not direction.

The DeFi builder's key fact: an AMM LP position is short convexity. A constant-product LP automatically sells the asset as its price rises and buys it as the price falls. It is always doing the opposite of a momentum trader, which is exactly what an option seller does when hedging. The fees it collects are the premium. Compare it to simply holding the tokens:

The LP loses a little whichever way the price goes and wins only when it stays put. That's a short straddle's payoff shape. Unit 6 names the shortfall impermanent loss and sharpens it into LVR. Paradigm's work on everlasting options and power perpetuals shows the trick runs the other way too: funding can make any payoff perpetual, including a convex one.

Leverage and margin

Leverage lets you hold a position bigger than your equity. The lender (a perp venue, a lending pool) protects itself with a margin threshold and liquidates you when you cross it. All of it follows from one equation:

leverage = notional / equity equity = collateral + unrealised PnL liquidate when equity < maintenance margin = mmr × notional

mmr is the maintenance margin ratio, often half the initial margin at maximum leverage. Everything in Units 7 and 9 is this line with different names.

Putting the four together

Here's how the pieces compose in things you'll build or analyse:

  • A perp venue is a futures market with funding instead of expiry and a margin engine running the equity equation for every account, every price update.
  • Ethena is a cash-and-carry on perps, so its yield is funding, its main risk is funding going negative, and its secondary risk is the venue holding the short leg.
  • An AMM pool is a book of short options written by LPs, with fees as premium and arbitrageurs as the counterparties exercising it.
  • A lending market is leverage with a margin threshold called the liquidation threshold, and liquidators instead of a margin desk.

When something new launches, find which of these it is underneath. It usually is one of them.
