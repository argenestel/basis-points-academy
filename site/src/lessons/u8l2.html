A peg is a promise that one token equals one dollar, and like every promise in Unit 1 it holds only as long as someone can and wants to enforce it. This lesson is about the three forces that defend a peg, and then about the most instructive failure in crypto history: Terra's UST, which showed exactly what happens when the thing backing the dollar is a bet on the dollar's own success. The $40B+ that vanished in a few days of May 2022 is a lesson you can trace loop by loop.

What defends a peg

  • Arbitrage. If anyone can mint a stablecoin for $1 of assets and redeem it for $1 of assets, then any market price away from $1 is free money, and arbitrageurs close the gap. This is the primary defence, and it's only as strong as the redemption access behind it (Unit 1).
  • Reserves. A peg-stability module or treasury that buys the coin below $1 and sells above it, directly defending the price. DAI's PSM swaps DAI for USDC near 1:1, which is why DAI barely moves.
  • Credibility. Beliefs about the first two. A peg is partly a Schelling point (Unit 1): it holds because everyone expects it to hold, and that expectation is itself stabilising, right up until it isn't.

Terra/UST: the setup

UST's arbitrage mechanism was elegant and fatal. You could always burn $1 of UST to mint $1 of newly created LUNA, and burn $1 of LUNA to mint one UST. So if UST traded at $0.98, you'd buy it cheap, burn it for $1 of LUNA, and sell the LUNA: a 2-cent profit that should push UST back to $1. The problem is what backs the dollar: LUNA, a volatile token whose entire value rested on UST's continued success. The backing was endogenous. On the way up this was a flywheel; on the way down it was a drain.

Two things loaded the gun. First, Anchor paid about 20% on UST deposits, a subsidised and unsustainable rate (Unit 1's yield decomposition: the payer was the Terra treasury, not the market), which pulled most of UST's supply into one place. Second, that meant a very large amount of UST was sitting ready to leave the moment the yield or the peg looked shaky.

The death spiral, loop by loop

The reflexive loop that broke UST

1. Triggerlarge UST withdrawals from Anchor and salesUST slips to $0.98
2. Arbitrage firesholders burn UST for $1 of LUNA and sell the LUNAthis mints new LUNA and dumps it
3. LUNA fallsmore LUNA supply + selling pressureLUNA's price drops
4. Each redemption needs more LUNA$1 of a cheaper LUNA = more tokens mintedsupply explodes
5. Confidence breakseveryone races to exit before LUNA goes to zeroback to step 2, faster
ResultLUNA hyperinflated from ~350M to trillions of tokens~$40B+ erased in days

The loop is self-accelerating: the mechanism that was supposed to restore the peg (mint LUNA, sell it) is the exact mechanism that destroys the backing. It was a run (Unit 1's Diamond–Dybvig) on a balance sheet whose only asset was belief in itself. Once belief went, there was nothing underneath.

Other peg events, for contrast

  • USDC and SVB (March 2023). USDC fell to about $0.88 when $3.3B of its reserve was stuck at a failed bank, and recovered to $1 within days once deposits were guaranteed. A liquidity scare on an exogenously-backed coin: painful, temporary, survivable, because the assets were real (Unit 1).
  • USDe on Binance (October 10, 2025). USDe traded far below $1 briefly on one venue because of thin local liquidity and venue-specific pricing, while holding near $1 on deeper venues. A pricing dislocation, not a backing failure, but it still caused liquidations for anyone using it as margin there (Unit 9).

The contrast with Terra is the whole point: exogenously-backed coins have scares and recoveries; the endogenously-backed one had a spiral and a zero.

Design lessons

  • Backing must be exogenous to the coin's own success. If the asset falls when the liability does, you have a spiral, not a stablecoin.
  • Redemption queues and fees slow runs but signal weakness. They buy time, at the cost of telling the market you're worried, which can accelerate the thing they're meant to prevent.
  • Price collateral on deep markets. A peg defended by selling collateral into a thin market (Unit 7's liquidation failures) isn't defended.
  • Unsustainable yield is a countdown. A subsidised yield concentrates supply in one place and sets the clock for the run (Unit 1).

Build it

Simulate a Terra-style system in Python: model UST demand shocks, a LUNA price that follows its market cap, and the mint/burn arbitrage. Find the redemption size at which the system can't recover (the point where step 4 of the spiral outruns incoming demand). Then add an exogenous reserve, a pot of real assets that buys UST below $1, and find the new breaking point. The gap between the two is the value of exogenous backing, measured in the size of shock your stablecoin can survive.
