The three strategies in this lesson, looping, option-selling vaults, and curated yield, all promise returns above the base rate, and all three turn a modest edge into a large position through leverage or risk transfer. They're where a lot of DeFi yield actually comes from, and where a lot of it violently disappears. This lesson works the loop math and then dwells on November 2025, when the combination of all three failed at once.

Looping

The most common leverage strategy: deposit yield-bearing collateral, borrow against it, redeposit the borrowed funds as more collateral, and repeat.

deposit collateral yielding y, borrow at rate b with loan-to-value λ, re-deposit, repeat max leverage ≈ 1 / (1 − λ) net APY ≈ y + (y − b) × (leverage − 1) λ = 0.9 → up to 10×; y = 12%, b = 9% → net ≈ 12% + 3% × 9 = 39% if b rises above y, the loop BLEEDS; if the collateral depegs 5% at 10×, equity −50%

The loop multiplies the spread (y − b) by the leverage. It also multiplies every risk: a small depeg at high leverage wipes out the equity.

The loop in two regimes

Good: y 12%, b 9%, 10×net ≈ 39%spectacular, while it lasts
Borrow spikes: b 15%net ≈ 12% + (−3%)×9 = −15%the loop now bleeds 15%/yr
Collateral depegs 5% at 10×equity −50%near-liquidation from a small move

Loops are profitable only while y > b and the collateral holds its peg. The cruelty is that both conditions break in exactly the stress events where everyone tries to unwind at once, so the loop that earned 39% calmly can lose half its equity in a day.

Option-selling vaults

DeFi option vaults (the 2021–22 style) sell covered calls or puts to market makers each week and pay out the premium as "yield." But selling options is being short volatility (Unit 1): you collect small, steady premiums most weeks, and occasionally take a large loss when the market moves sharply. The yield is real but it's compensation for tail risk, not free income, and a vault that's shown 12% for a year can give most of it back in one bad week. If you see a steady yield from an option vault, you're being paid to insure someone against a move that hasn't happened yet.

Curated yield and November 2025

When all three stack

November 2025 was the three strategies failing together. Stream Finance disclosed losses of about $93M, and its xUSD depegged. Vaults and stablecoins that had accepted xUSD as collateral or looped it took losses, including Elixir's deUSD. The ingredients: opaque yield collateral (nobody outside Stream knew what backed xUSD), hard-coded oracles that priced it at $1 so liquidations never fired (Unit 5, Unit 7), and recursive leverage between protocols (a loop spanning multiple platforms). Each protocol in the chain reported a healthy position; the system as a whole was one over-leveraged bet on an asset nobody had audited. The lesson for builders is blunt: know the collateral of your collateral, trace it down to something real (Unit 1's balance sheet), and refuse any collateral you can't audit, no matter how good the yield looks.

Build it

Write a loop simulator: inputs are the yield y(t), the borrow rate b(t), the loan-to-value λ, and a collateral price path. Output the equity over time and any liquidation events. Then replay a scenario where borrow rates spike to 30% for 3 days and the collateral depegs 3%. Watching a 39%-APY loop go to a liquidation in the simulator, from a move that would be survivable un-leveraged, is the most direct way to feel why looping is fragile precisely when it matters, and why the calm-market APY tells you almost nothing about the risk.
